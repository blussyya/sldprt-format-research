# EXP-050 — The off-cone display samples are chord points, not a metadata failure

**Date produced/captured:** 2026-09-15. **Status:** Open thread from EXP-048 resolved. No new invariant proposed; no parser change.

## 1. The thread this closes

EXP-048's closing note: *"localize off-cone samples on bottom face 35 and the 19 Pocket Wheel faces identified in EXP-047; distinguish chord/interpolation samples, stale display data and correspondence issues without changing original coordinates."*

EXP-047 scores each tag-4003 face with `coneMinus = MAX over vertices of |rho_i − |radius − h_i·tan(angle)||` against 1e-7 m, giving 27/47 passes. Because it is a **maximum**, a single deviant vertex fails an entire face, and the metric cannot separate "the stored surface is wrong" from "one sample is not on it". This experiment replaces the max with the per-vertex distribution and applies four discriminators. EXP-047's threshold is held at exactly 1e-7 m and no failure is rescued by loosening it.

---

## 2. Result

**20 failing faces: 19 Pocket Wheel, 1 USB hub bottom (face 35). 250 failing vertices in total.**

| discriminator | result |
|---|---|
| **D1** deviation sign | **250 inside the cone, 0 outside** |
| **D2** distance to a chord joining two on-cone vertices of the same face | **250 / 250 lie on such a chord**; worst distance **7.596e-9 m** |
| **D3** independent least-squares cone refit | **0 / 20 faces rescued** |
| **D4** co-owning surface tag of failing vertices | **247 × tag-4002 (cylinder)**, **3 × tag-4001 (plane)** |

### Interpretation

The deviation is **perfectly one-sided**. Random error, stale display data, or wrong stored parameters would deviate in both directions; a secant of a convex surface can only fall inside it. 250/0 is not noise.

Every failing vertex lies on a straight segment joining two vertices of the same face that *do* satisfy the stored cone, to within **7.6e-9 m worst case** — roughly 13× tighter than the 1e-7 m tolerance those same vertices fail by, and four orders of magnitude below their own residuals (up to 1.2e-4 m). They are interpolated points on a straight span, not independent surface samples.

Refitting a cone to each failing face's own vertices rescues **none** of them, so the stored parameters are not the problem: the failing vertices lie on chords **of the stored cone itself**.

All 250 sit on boundaries shared with a face of a *different* surface type. This is the mechanism EXP-046 already measured from the other direction — its 389 edge-ID groups with `different-sampling` between the two owning faces. Where a cone's boundary is shared with a cylinder or plane whose tessellation subdivides that edge more finely, the extra vertices are inserted **along the straight span**, so they fall inside the cone.

### Conclusion

**The cone metadata is correct on all 47 tag-4003 faces.** EXP-047's 27/47 is a measure of display-mesh interpolation, not of metadata validity. This is exactly the separation EXP-048 called for when it wrote *"Metadata validity and tessellation fidelity must be evaluated separately"* — the two are now separated, and the metadata side is clean.

Worth stating plainly: this **resolves** the discrepancy rather than explaining it away. The vertices genuinely are off the cone; they are off it for a known, measured, one-sided geometric reason that says nothing against the stored surface.

## 3. Scope and limits

1. **Not a parser change.** `parser/v0.2` is untouched; nothing is welded, moved, or re-sampled. Original coordinates are unmodified.
2. **No new invariant.** INV-020–024 are unaffected. EXP-047's and EXP-048's recorded numbers stand exactly as archived; only the *interpretation* of the 20 failures changes.
3. **Cone faces only.** tag-4003. Whether the same chord-insertion behaviour explains residuals on other surface tags is untested here.
4. **The chord test is local.** It searches the six nearest on-cone vertices of the same face for a spanning segment. A vertex could in principle lie on a chord not found by that search; none required it (250/250 matched).
5. **Why the exporter inserts these points is not established.** That it does, that they are one-sided, and that they sit on shared boundaries with a differently-tessellated neighbour are all measured. The reason for the subdivision mismatch is not.

## 4. Note on the controlled corpus

This experiment could not use the controlled C00–C12 models: **they contain no cone faces.** Per-model surface tags from `EXP045_RESULTS.json` show the controlled corpus covers only tag-4001 (plane) and tag-4002 (cylinder) — cubes, holes, fillets, chamfers and a shell produce nothing else. Tag-4003 occurs only in `distributor main boss rev a` (17), `Pocket Wheel` (20), `USB hub case BOTTOM` (8) and `Helical Bevel Gear` (2), and tags 4005/4006/4007/4009 never occur in a controlled model at all.

This is a **corpus gap, not a methodological choice**. Controlled models with known analytic ground truth are strictly preferable, and are used wherever the surface type allows (EXP-045's 94/94 STEP validation is entirely controlled). Closing the gap needs new controlled models exercising the untested tags — see `NEXT_QUESTIONS.md` NQ-030.
