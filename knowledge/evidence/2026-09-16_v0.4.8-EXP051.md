## 1. Why

Every check so far has been numerical: triangle counts, residual thresholds, edge-annotation partitions, invariant arithmetic. A mesh can satisfy all of those and still be visibly wrong — a triangulation that connects the right vertices in the wrong order produces correct counts and correct per-triangle normals while rendering as webbing, spikes or inside-out shells. Nothing in EXP-042–050 would have caught that.

This renders the parser's actual output and looks at it.

## 2. Method

A **self-contained software rasteriser**, written for this experiment: no three.js, no CDN, no external dependency of any kind. Orthographic isometric projection, z-buffer, flat shading from the geometric face normal, one hue per face index so face segmentation is visible, and — importantly — an overlay drawing every edge whose Block1 annotation is **nonzero** (the boundary edges of INV-021) directly on the mesh.

Input is `parser/v0.2`'s own `triangleIndices` and `edgeAnnotations`, consumed verbatim. Nothing is re-derived, welded, re-ordered or repaired. What the image shows is what the parser emits.

Two bugs in the rasteriser were found and fixed before the results below were accepted: an inverted depth test (which rendered back faces over front faces, showing the far side of the model) and a wrong field name in the edge overlay (`ids`/`token` instead of `vertices`/`id`, which silently drew no edges). Both were caught by looking at the first C10 image and noticing it showed a solid cube where a shell was expected. Recorded because they were rasteriser faults, not parser faults, and a reader comparing images to earlier drafts should know why they changed.

## 3. Result — all seven render correctly

| model | faces | triangles | boundary edges | what the image shows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C03 fillet | 7 | 36 | 50 | 1 mm fillet on exactly one vertical edge; the top face's corner correctly rounded to meet it |
| C04 hole | 7 | 152 | 160 | clean circular through-hole; cylinder wall shaded with correct facet banding; hole diameter reads as 50% of the face — 5 mm in a 10 mm cube |
| C07 two holes | 8 | 292 | 296 | two separate through-holes, both clean |
| C10 shell | 11 | 28 | 48 | **the decisive one** — 1 mm rim visible at the opening, hollow interior with inner walls visible through it, wall thickness ~10% of the edge |
| USB hub TOP | 68 | 4,704 | 2,842 | recognisable enclosure: walls, cutout, screw bosses, counterbored holes, lip |
| Pocket Wheel | 400 | 17,078 | 11,718 | sprocket with pocketed teeth, raised hub, central bore with a resolved **keyway notch** |
| Dekor | 375 | 15,282 | 15,366 | ornate fretwork panel with a phoenix cutout; fine detail intact, boundary edges tracing the design |

**No webbing, no stray triangles spanning unrelated vertices, no inverted or missing faces, no z-fighting artifacts.** The boundary-edge overlay lands on real face outlines in every model, which is an independent visual confirmation of INV-021's nonzero/zero partition: had the edge classification been wrong, the overlay would decorate the interiors of faces rather than their borders.

C10 is the strongest single check. A fan triangulation, a mis-ordered strip, or a dropped section would not produce a correctly hollowed box with a uniform 1 mm rim — it would close the opening or web across it.

Dekor is the most exotic: 311 of its 375 faces are **tag-4009**, the surface type with no external validation anywhere in this project (see NQ-030). It renders as coherent fine detail. That is not validation of the 4009 *metadata* — it says the display mesh for those faces is well-formed, nothing more.

## 4. Scope and limits

1. **This validates the display mesh, not the CAD surfaces.** Renders show the tessellation the file stores. They say nothing about B-rep fidelity, trim accuracy, or surface parameters.
2. **Visual inspection is not a metric.** No tolerance is asserted here. This complements the numerical work; it does not replace or strengthen it.
3. **One viewpoint per model.** A defect hidden behind self-occlusion from this isometric angle would not appear. Multi-angle rendering was not done.
4. **Colour is per face index**, chosen to make segmentation visible. It carries no semantic meaning and is not derived from surface tags.
5. **No invariant proposed and no parser change.** `parser/v0.2` is untouched.

## 5. Extension, 2026-09-20 — six viewpoints per model, four more models, and a mesh-closure check

§4.3 recorded "one viewpoint per model" as a limitation: a defect hidden behind self-occlusion from a single isometric angle would not appear. That gap is now closed. Appended rather than rewritten, per the append-only policy — §1–4 above stand as originally recorded.

### 5.1 Six views

Each model now renders as a **3×2 contact sheet** of six viewpoints, 520 px per panel, each labelled in-image:

| view | azimuth | elevation | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISO FRONT | 45° | 35.264° | the original view |
| ISO BACK | 225° | 35.264° | opposite side |
| ISO LEFT | 135° | 35.264° | third quadrant |
| ISO UNDER | 45° | −35.264° | underside at an angle |
| TOP | 45° | 89.9° | plan |
| BOTTOM | 45° | −89.9° | underside |

The back, under and bottom views exist specifically to expose geometry the front isometric occludes. Exact angles are archived in `v0.4.8/EXP051_RENDER_INDEX.json`.

**Four models added**: `USB hub case BOTTOM` (39 faces), `Helical Bevel Gear` (113), `distributor main boss rev a` (51), `PTC GE8080-8` (126). Eleven sheets, 66 renders total.

A rasteriser fault was fixed here too: the 3×5 label glyph bitmaps were malformed (17-character strings where 3×5 needs 15), rendering labels as garbage. Cosmetic, but the images are a committed artifact.

### 5.2 What the extra angles show

All eleven models remain coherent from every angle. Two are worth naming:

- **C10 shell** — the TOP view resolves the opening as a clean square annulus (the 1 mm rim) with the interior floor visible through it, and BOTTOM shows a solid closed base. Together these pin the shell topology from outside the isometric view that first demonstrated it.
- **Helical Bevel Gear** — correct helical tooth twist, splined shaft, and a hollow bore visible in both ISO UNDER and BOTTOM.

### 5.3 A visual doubt, checked rather than assumed

In the gear's isometric views, **background is visible between the bevel teeth** where a root-cone surface might be expected. That is the signature a missing face would produce, so it was tested rather than eyeballed.

Counting how many triangles share each exact-coordinate edge:

| model | faces | triangles | distinct edges | open (1 triangle) | manifold (2) | >2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pocket Wheel | 400 | 17,078 | 25,617 | **0** | 25,617 | 0 |
| USB hub TOP | 68 | 4,704 | 7,056 | **0** | 7,056 | 0 |
| C04 hole | 7 | 152 | 228 | **0** | 228 | 0 |
| C10 shell | 11 | 28 | 42 | **0** | 42 | 0 |
| Helical Bevel Gear | 113 | 6,042 | 9,079 | 32 | 9,047 | 0 |
| Dekor | 375 | 15,282 | 22,959 | 72 | 22,887 | 0 |

No edge anywhere is shared by more than two triangles. The gear's 32 open edges are 0.35% of its edges, Dekor's 72 are 0.31%.

Testing what those open edges are: **32/32 on the gear and 72/72 on Dekor are collinearly paired with another open edge** — each lies exactly along another, so the same physical edge is subdivided differently by the two faces that share it and coordinate-exact matching fails while the geometry coincides. On the gear, 16 of the 32 additionally have another vertex lying strictly in their interior (a T-junction), worst offset 8.33e-9 m.

This is precisely the phenomenon EXP-046 recorded from the other direction — its 389 `different-sampling` edge-ID groups and its note that *"7,048 triangle edges across six models have only one exact-coordinate mate occurrence, although the labeled boundaries pair"*, with the instruction not to weld them.

**Conclusion: no missing faces.** A hole large enough to see between gear teeth would need hundreds or thousands of open edges bounding it; 32, all collinearly paired, cannot bound any visible region. The gaps between the teeth are genuine open space in the geometry, seen through at that viewing angle.

### 5.4 Limits after this extension

1. §4's limits 1, 2, 4 and 5 are unchanged: this validates the display mesh not the CAD surfaces, asserts no tolerance, uses colour without semantic meaning, and proposes no invariant.
2. **§4.3's single-viewpoint limitation is resolved** for these eleven models at these six angles. Six orthographic views do not prove the absence of every possible defect, but self-occlusion from one angle no longer hides anything.
3. The closure check in §5.3 is a genuine numerical result, not a visual one, and is reported here because the visual inspection prompted it. It does not supersede EXP-046, which measured the same phenomenon more thoroughly.
